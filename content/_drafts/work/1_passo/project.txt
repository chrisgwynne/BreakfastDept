Title: Passo — a neighbourhood Italian that stopped taking bookings in DMs

----

Project_status: concept

----

Client: Italian restaurant

----

Summary: A concept for a neighbourhood Italian whose food was never the problem — the website was. It shows how I'd build a site where booking a table is the obvious next thing to do.

----

Challenge: Passo had built a loyal following on the strength of the food and word of mouth. The website, though, was an afterthought: a single page with a PDF menu, opening hours that were three months out of date, and no clear way to book. Bookings came through Instagram DMs, which meant someone had to sit on their phone during service. New customers couldn't tell whether Passo was open, where it was, or how to get a table.

----

Approach: We started with the one thing a hungry visitor actually wants to do — book a table — and built the page around it. Everything that didn't help that decision got cut. The menu went live as proper web pages rather than a PDF you have to pinch-to-zoom, so it reads well on a phone at a bus stop.

----

Strategy: The whole site is organised around a single question: "Can I eat here tonight?" Opening hours, location and a booking button sit above the fold. The story and the wine list come after, for the people who scroll. We wrote the copy in Passo's own voice — warm, unfussy, a bit funny — so the site sounds like the room feels.

----

Design_explanation: Plenty of white space, big appetising photography of actual dishes rather than stock, and a type choice that feels like a hand-lettered menu without being hard to read. The booking button follows you down the page on mobile so it's never more than a thumb away.

----

Build_explanation: A fast, static-feeling site with the booking widget embedded from the tool Passo already used, so no one had to learn a new system. The kitchen updates the menu themselves through a simple editor. It loads quickly on a mid-range phone on patchy signal, which is where most people were looking anyway.

----

Outcome: The site is built so bookings happen on the page rather than in the DMs, and a new customer can see at a glance what Passo is, where it is, and how to get a table — no scrolling required. That's the point of the concept: the good business was never in doubt, only the website.
